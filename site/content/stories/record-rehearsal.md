# How to record a band rehearsal, with a track for each of us

When we decided to record our rehearsals, I did the obvious thing and opened a DAW, the kind of recording program records are made in. We had [the mixer](/stories/digital-mixer) plugged into the laptop, a track for everyone, and a program that can do anything.

> **Drummer:** Are we recording?\
> **Me:** Hang on.\
> **Guitarist:** Can we play?\
> **Me:** Hang on. I'm looking for where it records.\
> **Singer:** I've forgotten the words.

## The DAW

A DAW is a combine harvester. It records, edits, mixes, adds effects and makes a finished song out of it all. At a rehearsal you need a small part of that, and the rest stands in your way.

We tried a few. Reaper was the worst for us: it can do anything, and every feature hides somewhere in its menus.

**In Reaper, looking for a feature is a career.**

Recording was only half of it. We left the DAW recording the whole evening, one long recording, two hours of it. After one rehearsal I sat down at home to send the band the good takes. Find where each take starts and ends in those two hours, select it on every track, export it, wait, find the file, copy it to the cloud. Then the next one.

**Two hours of rehearsal, then the rest of the night looking for the takes in it.**

## What a rehearsal needs

After a few evenings like that I knew what I wanted. Press Space and we're recording. Press it again to stop, hear what we got, and start the next take. Simple and fast came before everything else. The rest of the list was short too. A track for each of us, named, the same every week. Each take under its song's name, so you can find it in a month. Keep the good take and throw away the false start. Get the good ones to the band without anyone staying at the laptop afterwards.

Nothing on that list needs a DAW. So I wrote РЭХА, a free app for macOS and Windows that does that list and nothing much else.

## How it goes now

On the start screen there's a track for each of us, with a name and an input, filled in from last time.

Before a song, pick its name under **Next take** and press Space, which is **Record take**. Every track goes to its own WAV file as we play, it's safely on the disk every 30 seconds, and the laptop doesn't fall asleep halfway through the bridge.

**At a rehearsal you need one key, and it's the biggest one on the laptop.**

Space again is **Stop**, and the take opens straight away. Press play to hear it, then Space keeps it (**Save take**) and Esc throws it away (**Discard**). The next Space starts the next take. The take is called after its song and number, “Viasna 2”, so a month later you can still tell which take of which song it was. A take under 30 seconds with no star and no marks counts as a false start, and **Clear false starts** sweeps them all away at once.

The good take gets a star (★). At the end of the evening, **Send starred** copies every starred take to the cloud folder, a folder Google Drive or Dropbox already syncs. In **Settings › Folders**, under **What gets sent**, choose **The mix**, one stereo file for listening, or **The original tracks** for anyone who wants their part on its own. The band has the takes before we've packed the cables.

A song's page keeps every take of that song from every rehearsal, so you can hear how it grew, or how it was better in March.

## The other ways

A phone on the amp is enough if all you want is to settle arguments. It records the room as one sound, though, so nobody can take the band home without their own part, or hear the bass at all.

A recorder or a mixer that records to its own card gets you a track each without a laptop. Getting the takes to the band is the old story again: take the card out, copy, sort, name, upload.

A DAW is still the place to make a record. We used one for the track we released, with the drummer's MIDI that РЭХА recorded, and [that's a story of its own](/stories/metronome). At the rehearsal itself, РЭХА.

## What you need

A track for each musician means an input for each: an audio interface with enough inputs, or a digital mixer that works as one over USB, as ours does. A Mac or a Windows laptop. РЭХА itself is free.

**The laptop sits in the corner now, and nobody looks at it.**
