# Find where your band speeds up, bar by bar

In [the mixer story](/stories/digital-mixer) I said that recording settled our arguments. It settled most of them. Play a take back and everyone hears who came in late. Who sped up is harder, because a take sounds fast to whoever thought it was fast.

> **Guitarist:** Hear that? We're rushing.\
> **Drummer:** That's the song. It's meant to feel like that.\
> **Singer:** It felt fine to me.\
> **Me:** Let's play it back again.\
> **Guitarist:** We've played it back four times.

So I gave РЭХА a line for the tempo.

## A line for the tempo

Open a take in the player. Under the tracks there's **Master**, and in its row there's now a line that shows how fast we played, bar by bar.

The dashed line across the middle is the tempo the song should have, in beats a minute, or bpm. Around it is a grey band, 2% either way: about two and a half bpm for a song at 128. Inside the grey you're on tempo. The solid line is the band, one point for each bar.

Where we were outside the grey for two bars or more, the line turns amber and says by how much: “+6” where we ran ahead, “−5” where we dragged. Point at a bar and it tells you about that bar alone: “Bar 41 · 134 bpm (+6)”.

## The chorus

We tried it first on the song we always open with, at 128. The verses sat in the grey. Then the first chorus came, and the line climbed and turned amber: +6.

**Nobody was dragging. We were all rushing, together, as a band.**

The app works the tempo out from the drums. Our drummer plays a Roland TD-27, so РЭХА reads the notes the kit sends over USB, every hit with its exact time. Strictly speaking, then, the amber line was the drummer's.

> **Drummer:** I was following the guitars.\
> **Guitarist:** We were following you.

Both are probably true. In a chorus everyone leans forward, and whoever keeps time gets pulled along. The line can't tell you who started it. It tells you where and by how much, and that was enough for us. Now, before that chorus, the drummer looks up, and the guitarists look at the drummer. Our chorus is at +2 these days, inside the grey.

## The bridge

The bridge of that song is slower on purpose. The line turned amber there too: −9.

**РЭХА can't tell a slow bridge from a tired band. Some evenings neither can we.**

A song has one tempo in the app, so a change the song is meant to have shows up as drift. We know that amber in the bridge is ours on purpose and leave it.

## The song's tempo

The dashed line has to come from somewhere. We don't play to a click at rehearsals: the drummer has views on clicks, and that's a story of its own. A take still keeps the tempo that was in the metronome's row when we pressed **Record take**, which is the song's own, and the line measures us against that.

To give a song its tempo, our drummer taps it in. Press **Tap** in the app, and hits on any pad of the kit count as taps. Five are enough.

A song with no tempo can also take one from its last take: press **count it from the last take**, and the app listens to it and puts the tempo in the metronome's row. If the band sped up on that take, you get something in between, so pick a calm one.

A take of no song, a jam say, is measured against its own middle: half its bars were faster than that, half slower. The first jam we tried it on said “No steady beat found”.

**It's the most honest review our jams have had.**

## The dull bits

The app finds the beat in the drums: in the notes of the track with the drums icon when that track records an e-kit, and otherwise in its sound. So give the drum track its icon on the start screen. Without one, the app listens to the whole band, and the line comes out rougher.

A take shorter than four bars says “Too short to show the tempo”.

If the line is amber all over and that's more truth than you wanted, set **Drift shown past** in **Settings › Click & backing track** to 3% or 5%. There's also 1%, if your band enjoys that sort of thing.

**We still argue after a take. We just argue about bar 41 now.**
